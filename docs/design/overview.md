# Vision Statement

Ashwood County is a rural county where every road, building, business, and landmark feels like it existed before the zombie outbreak.

The world is designed to encourage exploration, survival, and environmental storytelling rather than checklist-based progression.

The player should gradually become familiar with the county's geography in the same way a local resident would.

# Identity

Ashwood County is intentionally isolated.

It is connected to the outside world primarily by Highway 16.

The county combines:

- rural farmland
- dense forests
- rivers
- lakes
- logging areas
- small settlements
- one principal town

Rather than being densely populated, much of the county consists of natural landscapes separated by long stretches of road.

This creates periods of quiet exploration between moments of danger.

# Progression Philosophy

The player should naturally expand their knowledge of the county.

Each new location introduces:

- new risks
- new resources
- new stories
- new landmarks

The world should become increasingly familiar rather than increasingly complicated.

---

# Design Goals

The county should feel:

- lived in
- interconnected
- believable
- memorable
- rewarding to explore

The player should remember locations by their identity rather than their loot.

Example:

> "The old quarry with the collapsed crane."

rather than

> "The place with lots of scrap."

---

# Long-Term Vision

By the end of development Ashwood County should feel like a place that existed before the player arrived.

The outbreak interrupted life.

It did not create the county.
